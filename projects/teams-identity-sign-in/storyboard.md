# Storyboard: Teams Identity and Sign-In

## Direction

Reference grammar: warm-white background, generous negative space, pale-blue tiles, centered product identity, short black labels, and progressive assembly. The recurring connective motif is one thin sign-in pulse that travels between scenes. It is never the main visual more than twice.

Narrative spine: one person's sign-in request starts unresolved, takes either a cloud-only or hybrid path, then becomes stronger when a second factor is added.

## Frame 1 - The identity question

**Time:** 0:00-0:06  
**Technique:** kinetic type plus Fluent glyph assembly  
**Primary visual:** one sign-in request

- A soft Teams-colored pulse enters from the left and stops at center.
- A Fluent person glyph, a row of credential dots, and a shield outline assemble around it.
- The words "WHO ARE YOU?" appear first; "HOW DO WE KNOW?" arrives as the shield closes.
- Keep Teams identification as a small, verified product mark or text label rather than a fabricated logo.

**VO:** Every Teams sign-in starts with one question: who are you, and how do we know?

## Frame 2 - Two paths

**Time:** 0:06-0:14  
**Technique:** hand-stitched SVG branch diagram  
**Primary visual:** a fork in the sign-in route

- The same request rises to a single junction.
- Two large pale-blue tiles separate horizontally: "Cloud-only" and "Hybrid."
- Each tile receives one simple symbol: cloud/person for cloud-only; building/cloud pair for hybrid.
- Avoid a comparison table. The branching motion itself explains the choice.

**VO:** Teams supports every identity model Microsoft 365 does - and it really comes down to two.

## Frame 3 - Cloud-only

**Time:** 0:14-0:24  
**Technique:** layered acrylic cloud boundary  
**Primary visual:** a direct cloud identity check

- A user card and credential token live entirely inside a translucent cloud boundary labeled "Microsoft Entra ID."
- The request moves directly to the Entra label and returns with a check.
- Use no on-premises hardware in this frame; the absence is the explanation.
- Recommended accurate VO: "First, cloud-only. Accounts live in Microsoft Entra ID, which handles sign-in in the cloud."

**VO status:** wording decision required. The supplied "name and password. That's it" line is visually supportable but too absolute for current Microsoft Entra authentication capabilities.

## Frame 4 - Hybrid

**Time:** 0:24-0:34  
**Technique:** isometric two-plane topology with staged routes  
**Primary visual:** on-premises directory connected to cloud identity

- Left plane: a generic Fluent building/server stack labeled "On-premises AD DS."
- Center bridge: the approved Microsoft Entra Connect or Entra Connect Sync service icon, unmodified and labeled.
- Right plane: a cloud boundary labeled "Microsoft Entra ID."
- The sync stream moves left to right. Then credential validation highlights one route at a time: Entra ID, AD DS via pass-through authentication, federated provider.
- Do not use the Entra Domain Services icon for on-premises AD DS.

**VO:** Second, hybrid. Accounts are managed in your on-premises Active Directory and synced up with Microsoft Entra Connect. Credentials get validated by Entra ID, AD DS, or a federated provider.

## Frame 5 - Add MFA

**Time:** 0:34-0:40.5  
**Technique:** macro material transformation  
**Primary visual:** a weak password link becoming a two-factor lock

- The two identity paths merge into a password-only link that visibly flexes and separates.
- A second ring snaps around the user identity, turning the connection into a stable lock.
- Three approved Fluent glyphs orbit briefly: code keypad, voice call, and phone/check for Microsoft Authenticator.
- Use the words "Microsoft Authenticator" next to the generic phone/check unless an approved Authenticator product mark is located. Do not synthesize the logo.

**VO:** Either way, passwords alone are the weak link - so add multifactor authentication. A code, a call, or the Microsoft Authenticator app.

## Frame 6 - Privilege first, everyone next

**Time:** 0:40.5-0:45.5  
**Technique:** sequential left-to-right rollout  
**Primary visual:** protection moving from administrators to all users

- An Admin accounts bubble appears first on the left.
- A blue arrow draws from left to right.
- A blue Everyone bubble appears on the right using the same approved People icon.
- Hold the completed state after the narration so the rollout direction has time to register.

**VO:** Start with your admin accounts. Then roll it out to everyone.

## Frame 7 - Approved end card

**Time:** 0:45.5-0:48  
**Technique:** supplied ProRes end card  
**Primary visual:** Microsoft 365 Copilot end card

- Play `M365-Copilot-endcard-type-2s-16-9.mov` full-frame and unmodified.
- Preserve its 2.50-second duration and animation while muting its supplied audio.
- Keep narration and music off the end card.

## Timing alternatives

1. **Approved working structure: 48 seconds.** S6 runs for five seconds, holds after narration, and the end card plays clean from 45.5 to 48.0 seconds.
2. **Shorter alternative: 45 seconds.** Requires compressing S6 to two seconds and is no longer preferred.

## Caption plan

- Static accessibility captions, maximum two lines, on a 90% opaque near-black strip.
- Keep captions off the end card.
- Do not repeat large on-screen headings verbatim in the caption area; the heading and caption will take turns carrying text.
